The Behind operator: ⍛

Behind it all is surely an idea so simple, so beautiful, that when we grasp it we will all say to each other: how could it have been otherwise? --John Archibald Wheeler Wheeler & Ford, 1998

The Behind operator (sometimes also referred to as Reverse Compose, or the Σ-combinator, if you are boring at parties) was introduced in the v20 release of Dyalog APL. Like Over we met earlier, it’s one of those patterns that seems pointless until the penny drops, and suddenly you see applications of it everywhere. Conor Hoekstra, @code_report, dubbed it (somewhat tongue-in-cheek, perhaps) the most significant addition to Dyalog APL since the introduction of trains in 2014.

Behind is easy enough to model in APL yourself:

Behind ← {⍺←⍵ ⋄ (⍺⍺ ⍺)⍵⍵ ⍵} ⍝ ⍛

So what does it do? In homage to Conor, here’s his example:

' ' ∘≠⍛/ 'mr owl ate my metal worm' ⍝ Remove spaces
Loading...

That’s saying “compress by the is-not-space binary mask”, which we could have spelled out as:

{⍵/⍨' '≠⍵} 'mr owl ate my metal worm' ⍝ Remove spaces
Loading...

One way to think of Behind is as a predicate-applicator. With compress, it becomes a filter (⍛/), as we just saw. If you “say” it, and omit the Behind, it becomes “not-equal-to-space compress”. Here’s the “first equals” predicate application to find the places where the first element of a vector occurs. Say it, spell it:

⊃⍛= 2 7 1 8 2 8 1 8 2 8 4 5 9 0 4 5 ⍝ first equals

We can ‘stack’ Behinds, too, combining the predicate application and filter aspects. From Adám’s video above, return all integers from a vector:

⌊⍛= 2.1 ¯5 99 3.14 4 1.3 ⍝ floor equals

Dyadic use¶

In dyadic use, instead of feeding the sole argument to both f and g, the left argument X is passed through f, and that result is then combined with the right argument Y using g. In other words, X f⍛g Y is (f X) g Y. It’s kind of like “flipping” the function f “behind”, making X the right argument to f.

We saw in the chapter on Rank the explicit definition of “sane” indexing, a.k.a “Select”:

Sane ← {(⊃⍺)⌷⍵}

which we can read as “enclose index”. With Behind, that’s just

Sane ← ⊃⍛⌷
1 2 3 Sane⍤0 99⊢'abcdefg'
