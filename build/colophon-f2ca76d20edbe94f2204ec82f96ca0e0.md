# Colophon

> I believe in the power of ideas, I believe in the power of books, but you have to give them time. --_Thomas Piketty_

This is the second edition of *Learning APL*, revised for Dyalog APL 21.0.

The book is written as a collection of [Jupyter](https://jupyter.org/) notebooks and built into a website with [Jupyter Book](https://jupyterbook.org/), which is powered by the [MyST](https://mystmd.org/) document engine. Every APL expression in the book was executed live against Dyalog APL 21.0 through the [Dyalog Jupyter kernel](https://github.com/Dyalog/dyalog-jupyter-kernel), so the results you see are real interpreter output rather than hand-typed.

APL code is set in APL385 Unicode, the classic monospaced APL typeface by Adrian Smith.

The source lives at [github.com/xpqz/learnapl](https://github.com/xpqz/learnapl); corrections and suggestions are welcome as issues there.

## Notes

This book started as a very personal project which grew out of my own obsessive notes during my own "learning APL" process. My APL knowledge at the time I started writing was nothing worth shouting about; I was very much learning on the job, so to speak. I rewrote large chunks several times as my knowledge improved, but looking at edition 1 today, several chapters are embarrassingly weak. Edition 1 started on the tail end of Dyalog v17, lived through the exciting times of the v18.0-18.2 releases to finally land on v19. Dyalog's now at v21, and a lot of things have changed, notably the introduction of Array Notation (APLAN) which came out in v20. The second edition unapologetically makes full use of all the latest features. It's an introduction to _modern_ APL, after all. 

My own situation has changed, too -- I wrote the first edition whilst working at IBM, and now I'm working for Dyalog. A case of poacher turned game-keeper, perhaps? Whilst I am now a Dyalog employee, this book isn't a "Dyalog product", it's very much still my personal labour of love. It's been very gratifying that so many readers have reached out to let me know that this book set them on the path of APL. I hope you'll like the second edition, too.
